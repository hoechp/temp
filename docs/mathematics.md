## A full union of three algebras

For real or rational coefficients, define

$$\mathcal A_K=K[i,j,\varepsilon]/(i^2+1,j^2-1,\varepsilon^2).$$

All generators commute. Every value has a unique expansion in the ordered basis

$$1,\ j,\ i,\ ij,\ \varepsilon,\ \varepsilon j,\ \varepsilon i,\ \varepsilon ij.$$

Complex rotation, split-complex geometry and dual first variations embed faithfully. Products produce meaningful mixed directions rather than converting one algebra into another. The four epsilon directions square to zero and have zero pairwise products. The two commuting directions `i` and `ij` both square to minus one; their product is `-j`.

| Subalgebra | Real/rational dimension | Distinctive structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C, generated by `i` | 2 | Circular phase; Gaussian rational coefficients in the exact backend |
| S, generated by `j` | 2 | Indefinite quadratic form, idempotents and null directions |
| D, generated by `eps` | 2 | First-order tangent information; a square-zero generator |
| CS | 4 | Bicomplex algebra; circular and split directions in one body |
| CD | 4 | Complex values and their complex directional derivatives |
| SD | 4 | Split geometry and its first variations |
| CSD | 8 | All these structures and mixed components together |

Here C over Q means Q[i], not all complex numbers. Selecting Q or binary64 coefficients is a separate decision from selecting a subalgebra.

## Arithmetic and inversion

Addition is componentwise; multiplication follows the defining relations. A direct basis product is zero if both factors contain epsilon, acquires a minus sign if both contain i, and otherwise combines the remaining generators. `Ultra` and `ExactUltra` implement this law directly.

The split idempotents

$$p_\pm=(1\pm j)/2,\qquad p_\pm^2=p_\pm,\quad p_+p_-=0,\quad p_++p_-=1$$

give useful computational coordinates:

$$X=(z_++\varepsilon w_+)p_++(z_-+\varepsilon w_-)p_-.$$

For `X=a+b*j+c*i+d*ij+eps*(e+f*j+g*i+h*ij)`,
`z±=(a±b)+i(c±d)` and `w±=(e±f)+i(g±h)`. This is a faithful representation of the whole union, useful for inversion and branch analysis. The defining basis remains equally important, especially when small mixed components must survive floating-point evaluation.

An element is invertible exactly when both `z+` and `z-` are nonzero. In each factor,

$$(z+\varepsilon w)^{-1}=z^{-1}-\varepsilon wz^{-2}.$$

`1+j`, `1-j` and `eps` are nonzero zero divisors. A matrix can be invertible even when every individual entry is a zero divisor: the matrix with rows `(p+,p-)` and `(p-,p+)` squares to the identity. Exact solvers therefore solve coefficient equations rather than assuming every nonzero pivot is a unit.

## Functions, branches and differentiation

For an analytic function on a specified local branch,

$$f(z+\varepsilon w)=f(z)+\varepsilon f'(z)w.$$

This yields derivatives of trigonometric, exponential, logarithmic and rational expressions. Logarithms have independent branch choices in the split factors. A generic unit has `n²` distinct complex-coefficient nth roots; a selected principal root is only one of them. At zero-body factors, root sets may become infinite or empty. Full root-family classification is an open API task.

The generator exponentials expose the three geometries:

$$e^{it}=\cos t+i\sin t,\quad e^{jt}=\cosh t+j\sinh t,\quad e^{\varepsilon t}=1+\varepsilon t.$$

Analytic lifting does not automatically cover every real-valued observation. Intensity and phase are treated explicitly as real differentiable maps. `abs(X)` is the Euclidean length of the eight stored coefficients: it returns a float and does not propagate a tangent. `abs2()`, `amplitude()` and `phase()` have different, documented observable semantics.

## Geometric meaning must be selected

For `z=x+u*y`, `u²=s`, the intrinsic form is `Q_s(z)=x²-s*y²`. It is positive definite for C, indefinite for S and degenerate for D. The matching angle is respectively a circular phase, rapidity with sector, or shear parameter with orientation. There is no single real angle that classifies every value of the full algebra.

`PlaneIsometry` supplies geometric transformations in these planes. `ModeOperator` extends the scalar state algebra with noncommuting operators; `Mobius` and `ProjectivePoint` supply projective geometry. Operator composition and homogeneous coordinates are separate constructions with explicit contracts. In particular, commutative scalar multiplication alone cannot model general noncommuting spatial rotations.

## What cannot be copied unchanged from the reals

| Real-number expectation | Obstruction | Useful replacement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Divide by every nonzero value | Nonzero zero divisors | Unit tests, solution families, homogeneous charts |
| A compatible total order | `i²=-1` contradicts ordered-field rules; nilpotents add further obstructions | Real observables, sector/cone relations or an explicitly chosen coefficient order |
| Positive multiplicative norm on all nonzero values | Products of nonzero values can be zero | Coordinate norm, intrinsic quadratic forms, operator norms and conditioning |
| A finite polar angle everywhere | Zero, null directions and sector boundaries | Sector-aware coordinates and projective charts |
| Every nonnegative rational has a rational square root | Q is not algebraically closed | Squared lengths, exact Cayley coordinates or an algebraic-number extension |
| All higher derivatives arise from one nilpotent | The entire epsilon ideal has square zero | Bicomplex finite steps for suitable analytic functions; new jet generators for exact higher jets |

The last row is an opportunity as well as a limit. Since `k=ij` is a second commuting imaginary unit,

$$-\frac{[j]f(x+ih+kh)}{h^2}=f''(x)+O(h^2)$$

for a real analytic scalar function evaluated on a consistent extension. Combining this with epsilon can access a third derivative numerically. This requires component-preserving algorithms and underflow control; see the [verified experiment](research/experiments.md#mixed-higher-derivatives). It does not require changing the existing eight-dimensional algebra, nor does it supply arbitrary-order exact jets.

## Coefficient domains change the algebraic situation

Over Q and R, division by two gives the split idempotents. Over Z these halves generally leave the integer lattice; over characteristic two they do not exist. A modular coefficient backend must derive its own unit and root rules instead of reusing the floating channel representation. The current `ModularRing` is a scalar foundation, not yet an eight-dimensional modular specialization. See [finite coefficients](research/experiments.md#finite-coefficients).
